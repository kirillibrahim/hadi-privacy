## The short version

Hadi keeps everything on your phone. There is no account, no server, no advertising and no analytics. The only thing that can ever leave your phone is an optional crash report, and it is **off until you switch it on**.

## What Hadi does with notifications

Hadi needs **Notification access** so it can hold notifications from the apps you choose during a focus block, then show them to you when the block ends.

For each notification it holds, Hadi saves:

- which app sent it,
- the sender or conversation name shown as the notification's title (for example a contact's name),
- the time it arrived.

Hadi **never reads or stores the text of your messages**. It does not look at notification content, attachments or images.

This information is stored only on your phone, in Hadi's private storage. It is used to build your Digest and your Stats. It is not uploaded, sold or shared.

## What else is stored on your phone

- Your focus profiles: name, colour, days, times, pause level and the apps you chose.
- Your settings: theme, language, default pause level, how long to keep digests, and whether crash reports are on.
- Counts and times for blocks (started, ended, ended early) used by Stats.

## How long it is kept

You choose how long Digests and Stats are kept, from 1 to 90 days (7 by default). Older blocks and the notifications held in them are deleted automatically. You can delete everything at any time with **Settings → Delete all data**. Uninstalling Hadi removes it too. Hadi turns off Android's cloud backup for its data, so it is not copied to your Google account.

## Crash reports (optional)

If you switch on **Send crash reports** in Settings, Hadi sends a report when the app crashes. It contains the error, where in the app it happened, and basic phone details (device model, Android version, app version). It does **not** contain your notifications, senders, profile names or anything you typed. Hadi removes those before sending. Reports are processed by our error-tracking provider, Sentry, only for fixing bugs. Switching the setting off stops reports immediately.

## Permissions Hadi asks for, and why

| Permission | Why |
| --- | --- |
| Notification access | Catch and hold notifications from the apps in your active profile. |
| Notifications (Android 13+) | Show Hadi's own alerts, such as "Work focus until 17:00" and the end-of-block summary. |
| Alarms (exact alarms) | Start and end blocks at the times you set. |
| Run at startup | Restore your schedule after the phone restarts. |
| Internet | Only to send optional crash reports. Hadi works fully offline. |
| See which apps are installed (launcher apps) | Show your apps in the picker so you can choose what to block. |

## Sharing

Hadi does not sell your data and does not share it with anyone. The only outside service is the optional crash reporting described above.

## Your choices

You can switch off Notification access in Android settings at any time (Hadi then blocks nothing), turn crash reports off, shorten how long digests are kept, or delete all data from Settings.
